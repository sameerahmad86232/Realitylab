# Phase 3 — cardboard support comparison, version 1

**Status: proposed, app-authored and physically untested.** No participant or physical outcome is recorded in this repository. This is a separate craft study, not a Meta-generated result or a construction instruction for the scanned object.

Live procedure and blank worksheet: `/trial/stability`.

## Question and limits

Does changing a cardboard support design change whether it tips or slides as a shared board is tilted? A wider support footprint may help resist tipping, but the comparison also changes base mass and may change friction, tape alignment and center-of-mass position. This is not an isolated width-only experiment, a precise force measurement or independent verification of a scanned object's behavior.

## Proposed setup

Use two pieces of thin cardboard, two similar paper straws or rolled-paper uprights, adhesive tape and one rigid, non-glass board/tray. A ruler is optional. Suggested preparation targets are bases around 4 × 4 cm and 8 × 8 cm, with similar uprights around 10 cm high. These are design targets, never prefilled as actual measurements. Use pre-sized pieces or folds and record deviations.

Tape an upright to each base center. Keep the uprights similar in height and attachment. Place both on the same level board on a table, away from edges. The original scanned object stays untouched and unpowered.

## Three paired observations

1. Start with the narrow base on the left and wide base on the right. Slowly raise one board edge. Stop at the first clear tipping/sliding event. At that same position, note each model's actual state. If uncertain, record uncertainty. Measure edge lift only if practical; otherwise leave it unmeasured.
2. Reset level and swap positions. Repeat the same tilt direction with the same board and assemblies. Record actual outcomes and confounds.
3. Reset level and swap back. Repeat and record. Do not keep changing the setup until the expected outcome appears.

The worksheet defaults every outcome to “Not tried.” A complete paired report requires both actual outcome selections and nonempty observation notes. It does not require the wider base to perform better. Export preserves planning fields, partial/failing reports, timestamps and explicit limitations. Even three completed self-reports remain `independently_verified: false`.

The local recorder makes no AI request and does not persist data beyond that page. Refreshing or leaving clears its notes. Export first to keep a copy. Automated tests fill synthetic notes only; they do not perform or validate this experiment.

## Human evidence still needed

Have someone actually prepare and try the apparatus. Keep the actual material/setup details, paired outcomes, failures and any agreed photos/video with the export. A knowledgeable reviewer should assess the setup and interpretation separately; a person's report alone is not independent verification. If the procedure is ambiguous, unstable or not useful, record that and revise it before calling it a demonstration.

User usefulness is a separate question: use `research/PHASE_1_PROTOCOL.md` with actual intended users. No number of generated or fixture-filled worksheets establishes user benefit, demand or hackathon eligibility.
