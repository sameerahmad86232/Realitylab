# Generated engineering dossier

Build & Learn now requests a complete dossier rather than only a small paper exercise. A ready lesson must include a validated construction overview, 2–6 assembly stages referencing actual scan component IDs, checks and missing specifications, plus a separate 3–7 step practical analogue. The construction sheet is downloadable as photo-free SVG with escaped model text. The source photograph, assembly stages, material cards, progress and observations form a coordinated presentation.

Construction is conceptual: the model explains typical manufacturing/formation and cannot verify hidden joints, material grades, tolerances or wiring from a photo. The practical project stays separate from the photographed object. Twelve allowed craft materials broaden the possible classroom mechanisms without introducing powered machinery or modifying the original. Natural objects are treated as structures/formation rather than manufactured assemblies.

Scan guidance covers structures/furniture, tools/mechanisms, electronic modules, appliances, optics, containers/packaging, textiles and natural structures. Optional focus prompts help users choose what to inspect; the photo remains primary evidence. These are domain instructions, not a claim that every object will be identified correctly. Simple objects can have fewer than three features; unfamiliar objects retain uncertain recognition.

Efficiency: learning requests omit unused coordinates, connection arrays, request metadata and consent fields from the provider prompt while retaining component evidence and principles. This reduces redundant source payload; the richer generation can still use more total tokens. Dossiers, five follow-ups and completion progress are cached for up to six scans in tab memory, avoiding a fresh paid request after ordinary in-app navigation. Refreshing clears the cache. The app does not persist these drafts. Existing blueprint modes need no inference.

Validation includes schema/reference checks, mandatory dossier on ready lessons, compact provider payload, escaped photo-free SVG exports and browser checks for construction output, source links, progress, downloads, mobile layout and accessibility. Real evaluation details are recorded separately; a handful of images is not a broad accuracy benchmark. Textiles, optics, containers and natural objects need further real evaluation.

## Exploratory live evaluation

October 28, 2026: three new real scans passed the frontend schema. The furniture scene was described as a cafe table with molded chairs (5 parts, 21.38s server time); the mouse as a Lenovo wired mouse (5 parts, 13.19s). A severely cropped double-beard screwdriver was not identified correctly: the model retained uncertain recognition and listed whistle/gouge/tool possibilities (5 features, 13.55s). This is a recognition failure handled as uncertainty, not evidence of universal tool support. Additional context or a wider view is needed. Existing fan, bicycle and compass records remain the earlier evaluation, not retested against the new prompt.

Two real fan dossier generations passed structural validation. Manual review rejected the first practical proposal as mechanically inconsistent: a taped-fixed straw was supposed to spin, and claimed airflow was unsupported. The prompt was tightened to require mechanical self-checks and prefer a simple static vane/stability demonstration. The second returned four construction stages and five practical steps (21.80s); it is clearly marked proposed and not physically tested. It is included as a recorded example, with provenance and no new inference on opening. Physical effectiveness still requires a human build; schema validity does not prove physical correctness.

The evaluation consumed five requests total, with no automatic retries. It does not establish accuracy for textiles, containers, optics or natural structures, or any universal manufacturing capability. The first proposal remains a documented failure rather than being counted as a successful practical build.
