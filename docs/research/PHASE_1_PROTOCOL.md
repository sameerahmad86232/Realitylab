# Phase 1 — maker demonstration study protocol

This is a study plan. There are no participants, measured gains or testimonials yet.

## Hypothesis

A maker preparing a hands-on demonstration can more readily connect one visible part to one working principle when its photograph, evidence labels, conceptual drawing and separate experiment proposal are linked. The interface may also confuse users or encourage trusting an incorrect explanation; record those failures.

## Pilot recruitment and task

Plan an initial five intended users who have actually prepared a maker demonstration. This small pilot is for discovering usability and usefulness problems, not statistically establishing learning gains. Do not recruit or send messages automatically.

Use the same task for each participant: “Prepare a short explanation of one visible principle in this familiar object. Point to the supporting evidence, distinguish a hidden-part hypothesis, and prepare a conceptual drawing. If you try the separate craft activity, record what actually happened.” Choose an object the participant can inspect without opening or powering it. Do not score a proposal as physically successful unless it was actually tried.

## Moderator script

1. Ask how the participant last prepared a similar demonstration and what was difficult. Capture their account in their own words; do not lead with product benefits.
2. Ask them to complete the task using their current workflow. Record actual start/end times, tools and whether the task was completed. If the baseline is skipped, leave its time blank; never estimate a comparison.
3. Let them use RealityLab with minimal coaching. Record start/end times, errors, abandoned steps, questions and assistance. If comparing conditions, alternate their order across participants and document order/practice effects.
4. Ask them to point to one observed feature, one hypothesis and one limit of the diagram. Have a knowledgeable reviewer assess those explanations against the actual object; the model is not its own ground truth.
5. Ask what they would trust, what they would check and whether they would use this for an actual upcoming demonstration. Record criticism and lack of interest too.
6. If a craft proposal is physically tried, record materials, deviations, expected effect, actual effect and failure conditions. Keep user reports separate from independently reviewed evidence.

## Data and reporting

Use pseudonymous participant IDs. The CSV template intentionally contains only headers. Leave unobserved fields blank; record an explicit “not tried” for physical outcomes. Store only information the participant agreed to record. Do not publish personal data or quotations without their agreement.

Report raw participant counts and times with exclusions/reasons. A model answer, completion checkbox, generated journal, fixture or simulated browser session is not a participant outcome. A five-person convenience sample does not establish demand, causal learning benefit or population accuracy.

## Proposed pilot decision criteria

These are future go/no-go targets, not results:

- At least four of five participants finish the source-linked explanation and drawing without moderator rescue; record all assistance and correctness separately.
- Every completed explanation has an independently reviewed evidence/hypothesis distinction; correctness matters more than task completion.
- No proposal is promoted as a tested activity without a physical trial and documented outcome.
- At least three participants identify an actual upcoming use and explain why their existing workflow is insufficient; treat politeness or hypothetical praise as weak evidence.
- If criteria fail, narrow or change the task before expanding object categories or adding models.

Any missing participant, baseline, reviewer or trial remains missing evidence; it does not become a passing result.
